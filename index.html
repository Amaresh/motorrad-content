Why garages switch

The problem is rarely one screen. It is the broken handoff between all of them.

A workshop does not break because bookings exist or because bills exist. It breaks when the booking never becomes a clean job card, the parts request never reaches procurement, the customer never hears back, and the owner only sees the damage at the end of the week.

What Tyming Chain replaces

Disconnected notes, verbal updates, and reactive firefighting.

Instead of separate notebooks for walk-ins, technician calls for updates, and manual reminders for pickups, the platform connects the intake desk, workshop floor, billing desk, and customer communication in one operating flow.

  • Appointments convert cleanly into arrival-ready job cards.
  • Workflow templates assign the right technician and expose stalled work instantly.
  • Inspections, parts usage, invoices, and follow-up messages stay attached to the same record.

Real workflow, not generic software

Built around the way a motorcycle workshop actually moves through the day.

The platform follows the same chain of events your staff already live through, then removes the weak links: missed intake details, unclear ownership, stalled approvals, missing parts, delayed pickups, and silent churn after delivery.

What managers start seeing

A better garage runs on cleaner signals.

  • Which bays are under pressure and which jobs are waiting on parts or approvals.
  • Where revenue is coming from: labour, parts, service type, and bike segment.
  • Which customers are drifting away before they disappear from the workshop entirely.
  • Which technicians are overloaded, underused, or handling the highest-value work.
Why it matters

Less time chasing status. More time fixing the underlying issue.

The point of the system is not to digitize clutter. It is to make the next decision obvious: call the customer, follow up on the vendor, free the bay, reassign the job, or send the reminder before the customer has to ask.
