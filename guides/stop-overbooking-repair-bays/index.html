How to stop overbooking repair bays

Overbooking is a math problem dressed up as a hospitality problem. Indian two-wheeler workshops promise morning slots until the whiteboard looks full, then spend noon apologising. This guide is the operational fix, then the Tyming Chain scheduler that holds it. Updated 7 Sep 2026.

Updated 7 Sep 2026

Count bay-hours, not names

Four bays open ten hours is not forty jobs. A general service that needs two hours consumes two bay-hours. An oil change that needs forty-five minutes consumes 0.75. If you write both as “morning”, you have not scheduled anything. Write the duration next to every job type on a tariff card the advisor can see without asking a senior tech.

A workable default for a compact Indian shop is 4 bays, 12 duration-aware weekday jobs, and 8 on Saturday. Those numbers are not a slogan. They are a capacity budget. If your general service really takes 150 minutes including QC, use 150 minutes. Lying to the book makes the floor lie to the customer.

Stop treating walk-ins as free

Walk-ins are revenue. They are also duration. Keep one bay or a slice of afternoon capacity marked for walk-ins instead of stacking them onto already promised bikes. When the slice is gone, the honest sentence is “we can take it at 4:30 or tomorrow 10:00”, not “leave it, we will squeeze”.

No-shows steal the same hours. Track them. A WhatsApp reminder the day before and the morning of the visit will not eliminate no-shows, but it turns a silent hole into a recoverable slot. Confirmations belong on the job, not in a group chat that nobody searches.

Assign the bay at booking, not at 11am

If the bay is chosen when the rider is already in reception, you are doing intake twice. Booking should name the bay, the job type, and the duration. The job card then inherits that slot. Technicians see a queue that matches the floor. Owners see occupancy without touring the lifts.

Model mix matters. An RE Classic 350 service and a KTM Duke 390 service are not interchangeable templates. If a job needs a specialist, the schedule has to know who is on shift. Otherwise you have a free bay and the wrong pair of hands.

What Tyming Chain does with those rules

Tyming Chain bay scheduling plans against real bay time on 4 bays, with configurable daily capacity (12 weekday, 8 Saturday), duration-aware slots, no-show tracking, walk-in awareness, calendar sync, and WhatsApp reminders the day before and the morning of. The floor view shows job card, technician, current task, and time on floor.

Use it as the clock, not as decoration. If the team still keeps a parallel whiteboard, the software will be ignored on the first rainy Monday. One book. One bay map. One reminder channel.

A 30-minute weekly review is enough to keep the numbers honest: how many jobs exceeded booked duration, how many walk-ins spilled into promised slots, how many no-shows converted to same-day fill. If general service keeps running long, change the template duration. Do not “try harder” on the same lie. Saturday capacity is lower (8 vs 12) because staffing and parts counters are thinner — treat that as a hard ceiling, not a challenge.

When a fleet drops a row of delivery scooters, book them as named duration on named bays or refuse the same-day promise. Heroics at 7pm are how QC gets skipped and invoices get argued. The scheduler is there so the owner can say no with a timestamp.
