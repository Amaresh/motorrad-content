## 4. Brand-by-Brand Analysis

### Michelin 🏆 Best All-Rounder
- **Top models:** Power GP2 (track/sport), Power 6 (sport/road), Road 6 (touring)
- **Technology:** 2CT+ dual compound (hard center, soft shoulders), MotoGP-derived RCT compound
- **Strengths:** Best wet weather performance, excellent longevity, fastest warm-up among hypersport
- **Weaknesses:** Power GP2 can feel nervous on cold/dusty roads; needs warmer conditions for peak grip
- **Price range:** $180–$280 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **Lifespan:** Power 6: 6,000–10,000 mi | Power GP2: 3,000–5,000 mi | Road 6: 10,000–15,000 mi
- **2026 launches:** Power 6 and Power GP2 now fully available; Anakee Road for ADV segment
- **Rider verdict:** "Stopped using Pirelli Supercorsas for these — grip and mileage is better" (Michelinman.com reviews)

### Pirelli 🏆 Best Track Performance
- **Top models:** Diablo Supercorsa SP V4 (track), Diablo Rosso IV (street sport), Angel GT II (touring)
- **Technology:** WSBK-derived compound, silica-rich high-performance formula
- **Strengths:** Fastest dry lap times, most transparent rider feedback, exceptional precision entering/exiting corners
- **Weaknesses:** Shorter lifespan vs Michelin, cold-running behavior needs improvement, less confidence in wet
- **Price range:** $170–$260 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **Lifespan:** Supercorsa SP: 2,500–4,000 mi | Rosso IV: 5,000–8,000 mi | Angel GT: 10,000–14,000 mi
- **2026 launches:** Diablo Rosso IV Corsa (bridge between Rosso IV and Supercorsa)
- **Rider verdict:** "Most transparent feedback of any tire in the comparison" (TyreReviews)

### Dunlop 🏆 Best Balanced Performance
- **Top models:** Sportmax Q5 (sport), Sportsmart MK4 (sport-touring), GPR300 (entry sport)
- **Technology:** Multi-tread compound technology, intuitive handling profile
- **Strengths:** Predictable handling, fast warm-up, excellent budget-to-premium range, great for Ninja 400/Z500 OEM
- **Weaknesses:** Some riders report cold tearing issues at higher pace; feedback less precise than Pirelli
- **Price range:** $140–$220 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **Lifespan:** Q5: 4,000–6,000 mi | Sportsmart MK4: 6,000–9,000 mi | GPR300: 7,000–10,000 mi
- **2026 launches:** Q5S (updated Q5 variant), Sportmax Q5A
- **Rider verdict:** "Great on slower bikes but feedback drops at higher pace" (Reddit r/Trackdays)

### Bridgestone 🏆 Best OEM Quality
- **Top models:** Battlax Hypersport S23 (sport), Racing Street RS11 (track), Battlax T33 (sport-touring)
- **Technology:** Pulse groove design, silica-rich compounds
- **Strengths:** Reliable OEM partner (Honda, Yamaha), consistent performance, good value
- **Weaknesses:** Less edge grip than Pirelli/Michelin, narrower performance window at extremes
- **Price range:** $150–$230 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **Lifespan:** S23: 5,000–8,000 mi | RS11: 3,000–5,000 mi | T33: 8,000–12,000 mi
- **2026 launches:** RACING BATTLAX V03 (track-only, new for European/North American markets)
- **Rider verdict:** "Solid baseline — you know what you're getting" (RevZilla community)

### Continental 🔥 Rising Challenger
- **Top models:** RaceAttack 2 Street (hypersport), SportAttack 4 (sport), ContiAttack SM 2 (supermoto/small sport)
- **Technology:** Zero-degree steel belt, MultiGrip compound, GripLimit feedback system
- **Strengths:** ContiAttack SM 2 uniquely serves 250cc–500cc segment; excellent value; strong wet grip
- **Weaknesses:** Smallest model range; less aftermarket recognition in US market
- **Price range:** $120–$200 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **Lifespan:** RaceAttack 2: 3,500–5,500 mi | SportAttack 4: 6,000–9,000 mi
- **2026 launches:** SportAttack 5 (successor to SA4)
- **Rider verdict:** "A rare offering aimed specifically at supermoto and lightweight sportbike classes" (Motorcycle.com)

### Metzeler 🏆 Best Sport-Touring Crossover
- **Top models:** Racetec RR K3 (track), Sportec M9 RR (sport), Cruisetec (cruiser)
- **Technology:** Interact™ technology, TT-heritage compound development
- **Strengths:** 2nd fastest in TyreReviews test, incredible M9 RR for fast road, strong TT pedigree
- **Weaknesses:** Higher price point in some markets, limited availability in US
- **Price range:** $160–$240 per tire (sportbike rear)
- **2026 insight:** Racetec RR K3 only 0.35s behind Pirelli Supercorsa in dry handling
