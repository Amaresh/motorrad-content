Detailed product map

One platform across the counter, the bay, the billing desk, and the owner’s dashboard.

Job cards drive parts, estimates, staff tasks, quality checks, invoices, reminders, and analytics. That means every team works from the same source of truth.

Platform overview

What ships inside the product.

The wiki behind the platform maps every important garage domain already: customers, vehicles, job cards, workflows, inspections, estimation, billing, inventory, vendors, employees, appointments, notifications, parts, analytics, and AI copilots. The product page below groups those domains into the decisions a workshop actually has to make.

01 · Appointments and reception

Keep bookings realistic and turn arrivals into ready-to-work job cards.

Appointments are planned against bay availability and service duration, so a day can be scheduled without overcommitting the workshop. When the customer arrives, the booking can convert directly into a job card with the right context already attached.

Business outcome

Fewer overbooked days, fewer manual re-entries at the desk, and cleaner intake for both walk-ins and booked customers.

What it handles

  • Date and time-slot scheduling by service type and bay.
  • Four-bay planning with daily capacity rules and no-show tracking.
  • Booked, walk-in, early-arrival, and late-arrival handling.
  • Reminder-ready customer and vehicle context before arrival.

Why operators care

  • Front-desk staff can promise only the slots the workshop can actually deliver.
  • Booked jobs arrive with fewer missing details and less time lost at reception.
  • Bay occupancy becomes predictable enough to protect space for walk-ins.
02 · Job cards and workshop execution

The job card becomes the operational spine of the entire workshop.

Each service interaction carries identity, customer, vehicle, complaint, priority, bay, technician, workflow steps, parts consumed, milestones, costs, and feedback. That gives the owner one place to see what is happening and why a vehicle has not moved.

Business outcome

Live pipeline visibility, clearer accountability, cleaner QC, and fewer bikes getting lost between verbal updates.

What it handles

  • Auto-numbered job cards with customer, vehicle, and service history context.
  • Workflow-driven progression from reception to in progress, quality check, and delivery.
  • Blocked states like awaiting parts and on hold, with timestamps and next actions.
  • Estimated versus actual labour and parts cost tracking on the same record.

Why operators care

  • Managers can filter instantly to active work, delayed work, or ready-for-delivery jobs.
  • Blocked jobs stop hiding in someone’s memory and start appearing as actionable work.
  • Turnaround time can be traced stage by stage instead of guessed after the fact.
03 · Employee app and technician coordination

Give technicians a faster working surface without drowning them in admin.

The employee app is designed for workshop reality: assigned tasks, job progress, approvals, and updates on affordable Android devices. It reflects the live workflow and helps staff move work forward without depending on constant verbal follow-up.

Business outcome

Less friction on the shop floor, better assignment clarity, and more reliable progress updates for service advisors and owners.

What it handles

  • Assigned work, workflow progress, and task-level visibility.
  • Status updates as jobs move through the workshop.
  • Leave and attendance flows linked to staffing visibility.
  • Mobile-first use on practical devices instead of desktop-only dependency.

Why operators care

  • Owners and advisors can see what is actually underway without walking bay to bay.
  • Technicians spend less time answering basic status questions.
  • Workload can be distributed with more confidence across the team.
04 · Estimates, parts, procurement, and billing

Move from quote to purchase order to invoice without losing the commercial trail.

Estimation, parts usage, inventory, vendor workflows, and billing belong to the same chain. The system keeps those transitions connected so the customer sees a clearer estimate, the workshop sees missing parts earlier, and the owner sees payment status without manual reconciliation.

Business outcome

Fewer pricing surprises, cleaner approvals, better stock discipline, and stronger cash visibility across the workshop.

What it handles

  • Structured estimates with labour and parts line items.
  • Customer approval before work or extra cost moves forward.
  • Inventory levels, vendor purchase orders, receiving, and parts-linked execution.
  • GST-ready invoices, discounts, partial payments, and refund tracking.

Why operators care

  • Estimate-to-actual variance becomes visible instead of quietly eroding trust.
  • Stock shortages appear before they create unnecessary bay blockage.
  • Outstanding payments can be monitored alongside workshop workload.
05 · Messaging, reminders, and customer retention

Keep customers informed before they have to chase the workshop.

Notifications are not treated as a side feature. They sit on top of operational events: estimate ready, job completed, pickup due, service reminder, payment received, or schedule confirmation. That keeps communication timely and tied to what actually changed.

Business outcome

Fewer inbound “what happened?” calls, smoother pickups, and stronger re-engagement of customers before they churn out of the cycle.

What it handles

  • Triggered messaging across WhatsApp, email, SMS, and in-app channels.
  • Template-based approvals, reminders, and delivery updates.
  • Delivery status tracking and communication history per customer.
  • Offer and promotion flows tied to customer segments and timing.

Why operators care

  • Customers stay informed without staff having to remember each manual follow-up.
  • Pickup and service reminders support repeat visits and lower no-show risk.
  • Communication becomes measurable, not anecdotal.
06 · Analytics and AI copilot

Turn daily garage data into operational and commercial decisions.

The analytics and AI layer sits on top of the same operational trail the workshop is already generating. Managers can inspect revenue mix, turnaround time, technician productivity, blocked-job age, at-risk customers, and stock pressure, then ask the system questions in natural language.

Business outcome

Faster management decisions, better prioritisation, and less time spent assembling reports just to understand where the day went.

What it handles

  • Revenue by labour, parts, service type, bike segment, and day of week.
  • Turnaround time, throughput, bay usage, and blocked-job analysis.
  • Customer retention, average days between visits, and at-risk customer lists.
  • Natural-language access for questions about operations, inventory, and performance.

Why operators care

  • Owners can spot bottlenecks, collection problems, and soft revenue decline sooner.
  • Service advisors can act on overdue approvals or pickups before they escalate.
  • The AI layer shortens the gap between “what is happening?” and “what should we do next?”
How the chain connects

The platform works because each operational event hands context to the next one.

That continuity is what makes the system useful. A booking knows the bay. A job card knows the complaint. The estimate knows the parts and labour. The invoice knows the approved work. The reminder knows the pickup status. The analytics know the full trail.
